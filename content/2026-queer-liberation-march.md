The 2nd annual Madison Queer Liberation March will be happening on June 13, 2026. Starting at 10 AM at [McPike Park](https://maps.app.goo.gl/QaqV9trde5K3s3nD8), we will march to the capitol with the following demands to Defend Bodily Autonomy.

### Protect Trans Youth

Trans youth in the United States are constantly under attack. Just in the last year, several bills were introduced in the Wisconsin legislature attacking trans youth's right to exist and be equal members of society. This ranges from attacking their access to youth sports ([AB 100](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/2025/proposals/ab100), [AB 102](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/2025/proposals/ab102)) to requiring deadnaming in schools ([AB 103](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/2025/proposals/ab103)) to preventing healthcare organizations from providing lifesaving care to minors ([AB 104](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/2025/proposals/reg/asm/bill/ab104)). While these bills are consistently vetoed by our liberal governor, we need more than just harm prevention. We need active harm reduction.

We need to hold our healthcare institutions accountable to provide comprehensive patient care. In response to unlawful political pressure from HHS, UW Health, Children’s Hospital Wisconsin, and reportedly other Wisconsin healthcare organizations "[paused](https://www.wmtv15news.com/2026/01/13/uw-health-childrens-wisconsin-pausing-gender-affirming-care-minors/)" gender-affirming care services to minor patients. Even today, despite a [federal judge ruling that HHS’s actions were unlawful](https://www.doj.state.or.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Judgment-Oregon-v-Kennedy.pdf), these institutions still do not offer this necessary care to their patients. They are afraid of losing essential Medicare and Medicaid funding from this tyrannical federal administration. We cannot allow fear to drive these organizations to harm their patients.

We need to fund queer-inclusive sexual health education in our schools. Currently, the only requirement for Sex-ed in Wisconsin is STI instruction. There is no requirement to provide information on human growth and development. However, *if* a school wants to discuss the topic, then they *must* stress abstinence with no other instruction being necessary. Instruction on gender identity, sexual orientation, and consent is encouraged by the [Department of Public Instruction](https://dpi.wi.gov/sites/default/files/imce/sspw/pdf/hgdunitintro.pdf), but is not mandated for any school. This is an unacceptable gap in public education that needs to be filled by qualified educators and social workers.

### Healthcare for All

Healthcare, including access to gender-affirming care, is a human right. Our for-profit insurance system is a cruel and extractive process that cuts off patients from the healthcare they need. Dismantling this system will be a long arduous journey. While there are proposals, like the [BadgerCare Public Option](https://wisconsinexaminer.com/2026/03/04/democrats-again-pitch-for-badgercare-public-option-to-expand-health-coverage/) that could widen healthcare options for some people, and we would generally support their passage, universal healthcare which is free at the point of use is the goal. Not only is it the most humane option, it is also the least expensive, as evidenced by every country that has implemented it.

We need to protect and expand reproductive healthcare access across the state. Currently, there are only four clinics throughout the state that perform abortion services, mostly concentrated in the southeast portion of the state. Ever since the Dobbs ruling, access has only gotten more difficult. While the Wisconsin Supreme Court rejected the original 1849 ban on abortions, [several obstacles still remain](https://core.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/1349/2023/04/WI-Abortion-Laws.pdf). Wisconsin does not allow [state, local, or federal funding](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/20/x/927) for programs that provide or promote abortion services or referrals. [Wisconsin law](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/253/10/3/c) also requires that physicians provide counseling in-person at least 24 hours prior to the procedure, requiring that the patient make at least two trips to the clinic, which is particularly burdensome for people that live far from available clinics. During the initial visit an [ultrasound and fetal heartbeat](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/253/10/3g), if present, must be shown to the patient, neither of which is medically necessary, and has its own separate costs. And finally, [minors are required to gain parental consent](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/48/vi/375/4) before seeking an abortion, though they can obtain a waiver from a judge. We need to repeal every last one of these unnecessary laws and expand access to these vital services by making it easier for clinics to provide and refer patients for abortion services. 

We need to end HIV stigmatization and criminalization. PrEP and PEP treatments are incredibly effective at preventing HIV transmission. [PrEP is 99% effective](https://savehivfunding.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Wisconsin_CDC-HIV-Prevention.pdf) at preventing HIV acquisition and yet only 22% of individuals that could benefit from the medication (those with indications for PrEP). The medical records of minors that take responsible steps towards maintaining their sexual health [can be monitored by their parents](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/2025/related/proposals/sb799). Despite no evidence that it promotes public safety, HIV transmission has been criminalized in the state of Wisconsin, including incredibly low-risk acts like [spitting on a person](https://www.hivjustice.net/cases/us-wisconsin-man-with-hiv-and-hepatitis-b-c-charged-with-two-felony-counts-of-second-degree-recklessly-endangering-safety-for-spitting-in-the-faces-of-two-hospital-security-officials/). We need to modernize Wisconsin’s laws, expand access to treatment options, and help end the still-ongoing HIV epidemic.

### Empower Queer Workers

Act 10 was and still is one of the most disastrous pieces of legislation for Wisconsin workers. It has cut off collective bargaining for tens of thousands of teachers, nurses, and other government employees. And even though a Dane county judge [overturned the law in December 2024](https://pbswisconsin.org/news-item/what-to-know-about-wisconsins-act-10-and-the-2024-court-battle-over-the-law/), the appellate courts and Wisconsin supreme court have stalled out the ruling for a year and a half with no end in sight. Whether through the courts or through legislation, Act 10 needs to be shut down in order for public sector employees to rightfully exercise their worker power.

Related to Act 10 is so called “Right-to-work”, another terrible law passed under Scott Walker that prevents labor unions from effectively organizing their members. Without worker buy-in, unions remain powerless. And employers constantly engage in anti-union practices that discourage that buy-in. While abolishing right-to-work would not end these practices, at least the struggle would ease once unions are officially formed. This would allow unions to focus on bargaining on behalf of their members instead of constantly needing to protect itself.

Importantly for trans workers specifically is the need to expand anti-discrimination protections to include gender-identity. Wisconsin was the first state in the union to ban workplace discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation when it amended the Wisconsin Fair Employment Act all the way back in 1982. But it now lags behind twenty-two other states that have expanded similar protections for trans workers. And although the recent US Supreme Court ruling on the [Bostock](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2019/17-1618) case has effectively expanded these protections at the federal level, the even more recent [Dobbs](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2021/19-1392) case, which overturned Roe v Wade, has shown us that relying on the courts alone to protect our rights is a risk we cannot afford to take. We need to expand the definition of discrimination to include gender-identity.

And above all, independent of any demands we make of our political representatives, we need to build effective worker institutions. This means organizing labor unions, building tenant power, and coming together with our neighbors to form community coalitions. This infrastructure is the foundation of worker power across all demographics.
